Merging the U.S. and Global databases onto a modern, ML-powered platform — with official downloads enriched by the Sabin Center's metadata, labels, and summaries — is exactly the foundation the field needed. This note is about a layer that complements that platform rather than competing with it.

The problem we want to help with

Your database is already being consulted indirectly, every day, through ChatGPT, Claude, and similar tools — and those tools routinely fabricate citations and holdings, attributing invented authority to sources like yours. Recent empirical work (Magesh et al., 2025) found that even retrieval-grounded legal AI hallucinates 17–33% of the time. For an authoritative, hand-curated database, that is a representation risk you do not control.

A demonstration for your review

We built a working, open-source demonstration of a Model Context Protocol (MCP) server — a standardized endpoint that AI clients connect to natively — designed around a single principle: the AI cannot fabricate. It is deployed (as a non-public research preview) at:

    https://mcp.openclimatelaw.org/mcp     (landing page: https://openclimatelaw.org)

It exposes twelve tools. The ones that matter most here enforce citation safety:
  - cite returns only the verbatim citation string of a previously retrieved case — the model is required to call this rather than write a citation from memory.
  - check_claim_support verifies that any quoted text appears verbatim in the retrieved decision text or summary.
  - find_relevant_passage resolves a claim to an exact passage of the decision — and returns "no match" rather than guess when nothing supports it.
  - attest_response scans a draft and flags any citation not present in the retrieved cases. A draft that cites something not in your database does not pass.
Alongside these: hybrid (full-text + semantic) search, full verbatim decision-text retrieval, a cross-jurisdiction "related cases" tool, and corpus statistics.

The net effect: a practitioner who asks an AI assistant to draft a memo on a climate case gets one whose every citation is traceable to your verbatim citation string and whose every quoted passage actually exists in the judgment — or the assistant declines.

How it sources data — and how we'd like to change it

The current preview was built against the prior climatecasechart.com. Going forward we would much rather consume your **official** distribution channel (the CSV / API / Hugging Face downloads from the relaunched platform), cleanly and within whatever terms you set, rather than scraping. We are not asserting any particular license over the litigation corpus — we would ask you and CPR to confirm the terms, and we will operate inside them. Every record carries explicit Sabin Center attribution and links back to the canonical page; the full source is open under the MIT license (github.com/jonashertner/openclimatelaw), so the whole pipeline is auditable.

What we can give back

Not hosting — CPR already runs excellent infrastructure. Two things we think are genuinely useful:
  1. An open, citation-safe MCP/agent interface to your official data, so the database is represented faithfully inside the LLM tools where it is increasingly consumed.
  2. A data-quality QA report (attached). In building the mirror we found, for example, that decision dates were in places derived from a metadata timestamp rather than the judgment date (we have a fix that re-derives them from the Decision-event timeline), and that the Climate Rights Database records largely duplicate your cases. These are offered as upstream-ready contributions, not criticisms.

Your control

The preview can be paused, modified, or taken down within hours at your request — no debate. And we would not move to your official data channel without your explicit go-ahead.
