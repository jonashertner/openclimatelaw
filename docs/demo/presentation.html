OpenClimateLaw

A citation-safe MCP layer over the Climate Litigation Database and Climate Change Laws of the World.

For the Sabin Center & Climate Policy Radar  ·  regenerative.law  ·  June 2026  ·  corpus as-of 2026-05-07
~20s. "Thanks for the time. This is a short, factual briefing: what we built over your data, what we found in it, and what we suggest — and you hold the veto throughout." Sets a matter-of-fact, substantive tone; not a sales pitch.
Context

Why this, and why now.

  • Practitioners increasingly run legal research through AI assistants — which fabricate citations and holdings 17–33% of the time, even when grounded (Stanford / Magesh et al., 2024).
  • MCP is the emerging open standard for AI tools to read external data, with attribution — supported across Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot.
  • No comparable citation-safe layer exists for climate litigation. Your two databases are the authoritative source — so the question is how they reach the AI era intact.
~30s. Facts, not alarm. The audience knows hallucination — the point is the *standard* (MCP) and that no safe layer exists for this field yet. Keep it brisk.
What it is

An MCP server — 16 tools, and one rule.

Discovery · verbatim retrieval & pinpoint · legislation · citation-graph · grounding/citation-safety · aggregates.

1

No fabricated citation

Every reference is your verbatim citation string — never model-built.

2

No fabricated quote

Every quote is verified against the actual judgment text.

3

No bluffing

It refuses to guess a pinpoint, and says when it can't ground a claim.

Open-source (MIT) — the guarantee is auditable, not asserted.

What we built on top — and the numbers

Structure your databases don't yet carry.

  • Anti-fabrication contract — global citation formats (US, ECLI, UK, AU/NZ, Canada, Ireland, South Africa, India, CJEU), verbatim quote-checking, and an LLM grounding judge for fabricated holdings.
  • Legislation layer + bridge — 5,347 CCLW laws ingested; 145 case↔law links by exact title + jurisdiction.
  • Structured outcomes — 1,606 of 3,742 decided cases (43%), confidence-gated, anchored to the latest disposition, quote-verified (0% upstream).
  • Structured parties — 92% of cases, plaintiff/defendant (0% upstream).
~40s. The "improvements" slide. Emphasise these are derived by us and labelled as ours (provenance), never presented as theirs. Each is also a patch we could contribute upstream. For Maria/Michal: outcomes + the bridge are the research-grade additions.

Known limitations — ours, stated plainly

What it does not do well yet.

  • Semantic pinpoint at scale — only ~3,000 of 4.98M passages are embedded (0.06%); lexical pinpoint works corpus-wide, semantic is limited (an infrastructure step).
  • Freshness — the snapshot is a pre-relaunch mirror (2026-05-07); an official feed would fix this.
  • Citation graph — heuristic, thin for flagship cases; not yet a feature.
  • International advisory opinions — the ICJ AO is present (52 docs) but hard to discover; ITLOS and the latest IACtHR are thin.
Stating our own gaps builds credibility with a careful audience. Don't apologise — just be accurate. For Maria: the ICJ AO IS in the corpus — it's discoverability, not absence. Each limitation maps to a "where your guidance helps" item.
