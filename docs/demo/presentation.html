The problem

AI is already answering climate-law questions over your data — and fabricating it.

  • Even RAG-grounded Lexis+ and Westlaw hallucinate 17–33% of the time (Stanford / Magesh et al.).
  • A fabricated citation or holding over your data is your reputation.
  • And a practitioner might file something that does not exist.
~25s. The hook — make it about THEIR name. This audience knows hallucination; don't lecture. The sharp point is the *third* one — a real filing. Pause after it, then pivot. Bridge: "So rather than describe a fix, we built one you can test."
Our response

A citation-safe layer you can test — not imagine.

  • An MCP server any AI client connects to natively — Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, Cursor.
  • Built on your published, CC-BY data; deliberately a non-public research preview.

The guarantee

Three things the AI cannot do.

1

Fabricate a citation

Every reference is your verbatim citation string — never constructed by the model.

2

Fabricate a quote

Every quoted passage is verified to appear in the actual judgment text.

3

Bluff

When it can't ground an answer it says so — and refuses to guess a pinpoint.

Open-source, so you can verify the guarantee yourself. Verifiability > veracity.

~40s. The persuasive core — recap what the demo just proved. These three rails are the whole pitch; let the slide breathe. The line that lands with lawyers: "an unverifiable answer can't be checked; a verifiable one always can." For Mike Burger: "open-source" means it's not trust-us — it's verify-us.
