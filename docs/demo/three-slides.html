How it helps users

A citation-safe research layer practitioners can rely on.

1Nothing fabricated

Every citation is a verbatim source string, every quote is verified, and it refuses to guess — answers go into a brief without re-checking.

2Both halves, bridged

5,027 cases and 5,347 laws in one tool: a case → the law it invokes → the verbatim statute text. No single source gives both.

3Structured doctrine

Not just find a case — its disposition, holdings, and the legal test applied. Answers "has this argument worked, where, and why."

4Where users already are

Native in any AI client (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini). For practitioners without Westlaw, the only citation-safe option for this field.

The value in one line: ask in natural language, get a grounded answer you can cite. Card 1 is the floor (trust); cards 2–3 are the depth (coverage + analysis); card 4 is reach. Lead with 1 and 3 for this audience.
What would increase value · observations to validate

Improvements in the source data that would unlock more.

Litigation · Sabin

  • A structured outcome + finality flag → "who won, finally" becomes filterable (we derived it; a native field would be canonical).
  • Merits vs procedural dates → "latest decision" stops being a docket order.
  • A clean claim_type vocabulary + dedup keys → reliable faceting; collapse Held ×4, Juliana ×5.
  • OCR fallback + a citation graph → recover ~21% of text-less docs; enable precedent links.

Legislation · CPR (CCLW)

  • An instrument-type field → filter actual statutes from policies, plans, NDCs.
  • The general litigated statutes (Clean Air Act, NEPA, ESA, APA) → the bridge connects US cases.
  • The case↔law concept mapping → comprehensive litigation↔legislation links (today 145, by exact-title match).
  • Per-language segmentation → multilingual statutes read in the right language.

From our pre-relaunch mirror — candidates to validate, not confirmed defects. Offered as a contribution; we're glad to share the record IDs and ship the fixes.

Frame: "things we noticed building on your data — offered to help." Not a critique. Lead with one each verbally (Sabin: structured outcome; CPR: the concept mapping) and leave the rest in the written report. Validate-first caveat matters.
